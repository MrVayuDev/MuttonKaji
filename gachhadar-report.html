Bijay Kumar Gachhadar — Acquitted by Special Court. CIAA Now Appealing to the Supreme Court.

A veteran of six parliamentary terms and five stints as Deputy Prime Minister, Bijay Kumar Gachhadar was charged with Rs 96.57 million in corruption for his role in the Lalita Niwas land scam — one of Nepal's largest corruption cases. In February 2024, the Special Court acquitted him, citing his actions were "policy decisions." The CIAA, rejecting the verdict, appealed to the Supreme Court in March 2025. The case exposes how political power in Nepal shields the powerful from accountability.

Section 01

Background & Political Profile

Bijay Kumar Gachhadar is a veteran Nepali politician who first won a parliamentary seat in 1991 and held it through six consecutive elections — 1991, 1994, 1999, 2008, 2013, and 2017 — representing Sunsari-3. Over three decades, he served as Deputy Prime Minister under governments led by Madhav Kumar Nepal, Baburam Bhattarai, KP Sharma Oli, Pushpa Kamal Dahal, and Sher Bahadur Deuba. He held ministerial portfolios across Home Affairs, Physical Infrastructure, Water Resources, and Information and Communications a total of 13 times.

He was a senior figure within the Nepali Congress (NC) before breaking away to join — and later fracture — the Madhesi Janadhikar Forum (MJF) during the Madhesi rights movement. His career has been defined not just by political longevity, but by persistent allegations of opportunism, corruption, and the exploitation of institutional power for personal gain.

In 2022, Gachhadar lost his parliamentary seat for the first time in over 30 years, defeated in Sunsari-3 by CPN-UML's Bhagwati Chaudhary by more than 5,000 votes — an electoral consequence that many observers linked to the corruption charges that had hounded him since 2020.

Section 02 — Primary Scandal

The Lalita Niwas / Baluwatar Land Scam

This is the most serious criminal and legal matter against Gachhadar, and one of the largest corruption cases in Nepal's post-2006 history.

Case BackgroundWhat Happened

In 2010, Gachhadar served as Deputy Prime Minister and Minister for Physical Infrastructure in the Madhav Kumar Nepal government. His ministry submitted proposals to the Cabinet recommending that government land plots at Baluwatar — near the Prime Minister's residence at Lalita Niwas — be registered in the names of dozens of individuals, many of them alleged fake tenants, ostensibly to expand the PM's residence.

Critics and investigators noted that decisions on land reform and tenant classification should have gone through the Land Reforms Ministry — not the Physical Infrastructure Ministry — making Gachhadar's ministry's role jurisdictionally improper from the outset. The scheme resulted in 136 ropanis of government land being illegally registered in private citizens' names.

"I have not personally harmed the Nepal government by taking advantage. I only submitted the proposal of the ministry to the Council of Ministers." — Bijay Kumar Gachhadar, Press Conference after CIAA indictment

The scandal was uncovered by a government-appointed investigation committee headed by former secretary Sharada Prasad Trital, whose report triggered the Commission for Investigation of Abuse of Authority (CIAA) to launch formal proceedings in early 2019.

On February 5, 2020, the CIAA filed corruption cases against 175 individuals at Nepal's Special Court, including former ministers, businessmen, middlemen, and land beneficiaries. The case is among the most expansive corruption prosecutions in Nepal's modern history.

Charges Against Gachhadar SpecificallyFinancial & Criminal Demands

The CIAA demanded that Gachhadar pay Rs 96.5 million (approximately NPR 9.65 crore) — representing the alleged loss to the state — and serve a jail sentence of eight to ten years. This placed him among the more seriously charged political figures in the case.

For comparison, former middlemen Ram Kumar Subedi faced the highest charges of Rs 525 million, and businessman Min Bahadur Gurung (owner of Bhatbhateni Supermarket) faced Rs 500 million. Gachhadar's co-accused former minister Dambar Shrestha faced identical charges of Rs 96.57 million.

As a sitting Member of Parliament at the time, Gachhadar was automatically suspended from the House of Representatives upon the filing of the corruption case — as mandated by Nepal's Corruption Prevention Act of 2002.

Section 03 — Court Conduct

Absconding from Justice: Defying Court Summons

In March 2020, the Special Court issued a summons requiring Gachhadar to appear within 15 days. He failed to comply. For approximately one full year following the issuance of summons, he remained absent from legal proceedings and was formally categorized as "absconding" — a classification reserved for defendants who evade judicial process.

He finally appeared before the Special Court only after a significant political development: the day after his party leader, Sher Bahadur Deuba, was sworn in as Prime Minister in July 2021. The politically calculated timing of his appearance — coinciding with a change in government to one led by a figure sympathetic to him — raised immediate questions about whether he was using political connections to shield himself from prosecution.

Gachhadar's appearance at court — timed one day after Deuba became Prime Minister — was widely interpreted as a calculated political maneuver to appear under a friendly government umbrella. — Media Analysis, Nepali Press, July 2021

The Special Court released him on a bail of Rs 1 million — one of the highest bail amounts set among the 175 defendants in the Lalita Niwas case.

Section 04 — Political Treachery

Splitting the Madhesi Movement: The MJF Betrayal

Following the People's Movement of 2006, Gachhadar left the Nepali Congress to join the Madhesi Janadhikar Forum (MJF), a political formation fighting for the rights, proportional representation, and federalist inclusion of the Madhesi people of Nepal's Terai region. His tenure within the MJF, however, is remembered in the Madhesi community as one of calculated destruction.

Observers and internal party members accused Gachhadar of unilaterally deciding to back the UML-NC combine against the MJF's official position, bargaining a ministerial portfolio for himself in exchange. He was accused of distributing money to win legislators to his side and using political contacts to pressure the Prime Minister to include him in cabinet against his own party's official stance.

Institutional DamageFormal Party Split

Gachhadar ultimately registered a breakaway faction — Madhesi Janadhikar Forum-Nepal (MJF-N) — at the Election Commission, officially splitting what had been the fourth-largest party in the Constituent Assembly. MJF-Democratic members who later resigned accused Gachhadar and other leaders of "cheating the Madhesi people." His actions were widely seen as permanently weakening the Madhesi political bloc at a critical moment in Nepal's constitution-writing process.

He was also formally expelled from the original Nepali Congress in March 2008, having already abandoned the party to join the Peoples' Rights Forum. His subsequent return to Nepali Congress — after fracturing the MJF — completed a full circle of party-switching that critics described as driven entirely by personal political calculation.

Section 05 — Institutional Failure

Selective Prosecution: The Big Fish that Swam Free

The CIAA's conduct in the Lalita Niwas case itself drew sharp criticism from civil society. Despite the illegal land transfers taking place during the tenures of Prime Ministers Madhav Kumar Nepal and Baburam Bhattarai, neither was indicted. The CIAA concluded that their decisions were "collective policy decisions of the Cabinet" and cited a Supreme Court ruling to justify not prosecuting them.

Khem Raj Regmi, President of Transparency International Nepal, publicly criticized this disparity, arguing that those who took the fundamental decision to illegally transfer land were left untouched, while those who implemented lower-level steps — like Gachhadar — faced prosecution. This criticism cuts both ways: it raises questions both about the selective nature of Nepal's anti-corruption mechanism and about whether Gachhadar used the relative fairness argument to deflect from his own documented culpability.

Those who actually decided to transfer the land illegally were spared prosecution, while implementers faced the full weight of the law. — Khem Raj Regmi, President, Transparency International Nepal

Section 08 — The Acquittal & What It Reveals

Acquitted: How Nepal's Political Class Escapes Accountability

On February 15, 2024, Nepal's Special Court acquitted Bijay Kumar Gachhadar — along with former ministers Sanjay Kumar Shah, Dambar Bahadur Shrestha, and Chandradev Joshi — in the Lalita Niwas land scam. The court's reasoning: their actions constituted "policy decisions" made in their ministerial capacities, placing them outside the CIAA's investigative purview.

The "Policy Decision" Escape HatchWhy the Acquittal Is Itself the Scandal

The Special Court's logic — that ministers who formally submitted proposals that resulted in 136 ropani of government land being fraudulently transferred cannot be held criminally liable because they were acting in a "policy capacity" — amounts to a near-blanket immunity for political leaders. Over 100 businessmen, middlemen, bureaucrats, and fake tenants were convicted. The people who ordered and enabled the scheme at the top walked free.

Khem Raj Regmi of Transparency International Nepal had already noted this pattern when the case was filed: those who actually decided to transfer the land illegally were left untouched, while implementers faced the full weight of the law. The Special Court's verdict made this impunity legally official.

Those who actually decided to transfer the land illegally were spared prosecution, while implementers faced the full weight of the law. — Khem Raj Regmi, President, Transparency International Nepal — vindicated by the acquittal

The CIAA refused to accept this outcome. On March 5, 2025, it filed a formal appeal with the Supreme Court, arguing the Special Court's verdict was flawed and failed to adequately analyze the evidence presented. The appeal seeks to overturn the acquittals of Gachhadar and the other ministers. As of February 2026, the Supreme Court has not issued a ruling. The final legal outcome remains pending.

What cannot be appealed — and what the court's verdict cannot erase — is the documented factual record: Gachhadar's ministry submitted the proposal. The Cabinet approved it. 136 ropani of government land was illegally registered in private names. Hundreds of millions of rupees of state property was lost. The politicians who made it happen were acquitted. The pattern is not unique to Gachhadar — it has defined Nepali political accountability for decades. The Lalita Niwas case, even in its acquittal phase, is a textbook illustration of why the Gen-Z generation eventually took to the streets.

Key Conviction vs. Acquittal SplitWho Was Punished, Who Was Not

Convicted: Ram Kumar Subedi (middleman, Rs 525M charges), Min Bahadur Gurung (Bhatbhateni owner, Rs 500M), former secretaries Chhabi Raj Pant and Dip Basnyat — bureaucrats and businessmen who executed the fraud.

Acquitted: Bijay Kumar Gachhadar (Minister who submitted the Cabinet proposal), Sanjay Kumar Shah (State Minister), Dambar Bahadur Shrestha (Land Reforms Minister), Chandradev Joshi (Land Reforms Minister) — the politicians at the top of the chain of command.

The court also ordered confiscation and return of illegally transferred land from 65 individuals. The land exists. The fraud is proven. The political architects are free.
